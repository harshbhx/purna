# What the numbers mean

Each session gives a few numbers. Here they are in plain words first, then exactly how they're worked out. They describe one short practice, not the person.

## The four results on the report card

| Result | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **Left vs right** | How many whole diyas were found on each half of the table, for example "Left 4/6, right 6/6" |
| **Chipped diyas** | How many chipped diyas were lit by mistake, split by which side their gap was on |
| **Search order** | How tidy the search was: a number from 0 (scattered) to 1 (row by row), how often the path crossed itself, and how often a lit diya was pinched again |
| **Head turn** | How far the head turned to the left at most, in degrees |

Every report also says: "Practice result, not a diagnosis."

## Where the head pointed

After the replay, the table lights up where the head pointed during the task: brighter strips are where it pointed longest. On the family site and in the therapist view, the replay shows the same warm light. A side that stays dark was never looked at, even if a diya there was found by chance. The map splits the table into 14 strips of 5 cm and counts where the centre of the view landed, about ten times a second.

## Reading them sensibly

- **One session is a snapshot.** Tiredness, mood and the day all play a part. Look at the trend over weeks.
- **The shaded band** on the trend is the person's usual change from one session to the next. A change inside it may be chance.
- **Fewer targets, bigger jumps.** On Standard there are 6 whole diyas per side, so one miss changes the left score by about 17 points.
- **Ask the therapist** what the numbers mean for this person.

## For therapists: definitions

All positions are in centimetres on the table, with x negative to the left. A diya counts as left if x < −3 cm and right if x > 3 cm; nothing is placed in between.

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Left / right hits | Whole diyas lit on each side, out of the whole diyas on that side |
| Egocentric asymmetry | (right hits − left hits) / (left hits + right hits); 0 if nothing lit. Positive means a right bias |
| Chipped lit, gap left / right | Chipped diyas pinched, counted by the side their gap is on |
| Allocentric asymmetry | Chipped lit with gap left − chipped lit with gap right. Positive means missing the left of objects |
| Centre of Cancellation (CoC) | Mean over lit whole diyas of 2·(x − xmin)/(xmax − xmin) − 1, using the table's usable edges; −1 to +1, positive = found mostly on the right; empty if nothing lit |
| Best r | The larger of \|Pearson r\| between lighting order and x, and between order and depth; empty with fewer than 3 lit |
| Crossings | Proper intersections between non-adjacent segments of the lit path |
| Revisits | Pinches on a diya already lit |
| Time by third | Mean seconds to find a diya in the left, centre and right thirds (time since the previous find or the start) |
| Hints | Hint 1 (bell + pulse) and hint 2 (sweep) counts |
| Head turn | Maximum yaw left and right; share of samples turned more than 5° left. Sampled at most 10 times a second |

The scoring follows published cancellation tests: the Ota test and the Apples Test separate egocentric from allocentric neglect with targets that have a gap on one side, and the Centre of Cancellation summarises spatial bias (Rorden & Karnath). Published cut-offs for those tests **don't** apply to Purna; see [Guide for therapists](#therapists).

## How we checked the measures

Three scripted searchers played the real Purna engine on 200 table layouts each: one who searches the whole table, one who misses the left of the table, and one who misses the left of each diya. The measures told them apart on every layout. **These are simulated players, not patients.** It shows the arithmetic separates search styles; it says nothing yet about real people.
